# Research

## Workflow

1. **Define the question.** Identify the decision the research should support, scope, time sensitivity, and required depth.
2. **Find primary evidence.** Prefer official documentation, original papers, datasets, standards, and first-party announcements for factual claims.
3. **Cross-check.** Confirm consequential or surprising claims with independent reliable sources where possible. Check publication dates and whether evidence is still current.
4. **Evaluate sources.** Separate direct evidence from commentary, marketing, speculation, and unverified anecdotes.
5. **Resolve disagreement.** Explain meaningful conflicts, differences in definitions, or gaps in the evidence.
6. **Synthesize.** Answer the actual question first, then provide the reasoning and practical implications.
7. **Cite precisely.** Attach sources to the claims they support. Never fabricate quotations, links, dates, or citations.
8. **State limits.** Mark unknowns, assumptions, and evidence that could not be independently verified.

## Guardrails

- Do not treat search ranking as proof of reliability.
- Do not present outdated information as current.
- Do not cherry-pick evidence to support a predetermined conclusion.
- Do not expose private or sensitive information gathered without authorization.
- For high-stakes topics, communicate uncertainty and encourage appropriate professional review.

## Completion checklist

- [ ] Question and scope are clear.
- [ ] Important claims have appropriate sources.
- [ ] Dates and source quality have been checked.
- [ ] Uncertainty and limitations are explicit.
